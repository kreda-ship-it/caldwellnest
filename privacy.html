01 Who this covers

This Privacy Policy applies to Nestrel, a campus platform for verified Caldwell University students, operated by KR, doing business as Amahle Digital Creatives ("we," "us," "our"). Nestrel is independent: it is not owned, operated, or endorsed by Caldwell University.

The standard we hold ourselves to: FERPA

FERPA (the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act) is the federal law that protects students' education records — things like grades, transcripts, and class schedules kept by a school. It doesn't legally apply to Nestrel: we aren't a school, and we don't receive any records from Caldwell University. What we hold is information people give us directly through Nestrel.

That's also why you won't see us call Nestrel "FERPA compliant" or "FERPA certified". The law governs schools, and there is no official certification an outside app can earn — so any app claiming one is overstating it. What we can promise, and do, is that we voluntarily meet FERPA's standards:

What FERPA gives studentsWhat Nestrel does
The right to see your recordsAsk for a copy of everything we hold about you, and get it within 45 days — FERPA's own deadline (section 08).
The right to ask for correctionsEdit most things yourself; for anything else, ask us. If we decline, we explain why in writing (section 08).
Consent before records are sharedWe share only with the people and services named in sections 05 and 06, and nowhere else without your consent — except where the law or someone's safety requires it.
A record of disclosuresWe log any disclosure outside those categories, and you can ask to see it (section 05).
Limits on "directory information"Only your public profile is visible to other students, and you choose most of what's on it.
Safeguards and staff access limitsAccess rules enforced by the database itself, admin access limited to named accounts, and an activity log (section 09).

Why go further than the law asks? You sign up with your university email, and Nestrel holds your messages and campus activity — information that deserves that care whatever the law technically requires. In practice, it also means:

  • We use your information only for the purposes we list (section 03), and nothing broader.
  • We don't sell it, advertise with it, or pass it on beyond the people and services named in sections 05 and 06.
  • We collect only what we need to verify you and run the platform (section 02).
  • You can see, correct, and delete your information, with a set response time (section 08).
  • We keep information for stated periods (section 07) and protect it with real safeguards (section 09).

If a university ever shares its own student records with us, we'll accept them only under a written agreement that gives the university control over how those records are used, kept, and destroyed — and we'll update this policy before that happens.

03 How we use it

  • To verify you're a current Caldwell student and give you access.
  • To operate core features: listings, marketplace, textbooks, free items, clubs and organizations, events, saved items, and messaging.
  • To display your listings and public profile to other verified students.
  • To let club officers run their events — RSVP lists, door check-in, and feedback summaries (see section 05).
  • To show club officers how their club is doing, using totals only — how many students viewed, saved, RSVP'd to and attended each event, average ratings once at least five people have rated, and how many new followers the club gained. The record of who opened an event is used only to count each person once; officers never see it (see section 05).
  • To keep you up to date in your Activity feed — for example when an event you're going to changes or is cancelled, when photos from an event you went to are shared, or when a poll you voted in closes. These updates are worked out from information we already hold; nothing extra is collected to make them.
  • To keep the platform safe — moderating content, investigating abuse, and enforcing our Terms.
  • To understand how the platform is used and improve it, using totals and counts (e.g., which categories are active) rather than tracking what individual students do.
  • To contact you about your account, security, or important service changes.
  • To meet legal obligations, such as responding to a valid court order (see section 05).

During the beta

While Nestrel is in beta we look at totals and counts — how many listings were posted, how many people opened events — to find bugs and decide what to build next. The beta is a product test, not a research study: we don't run experiments on individual students or publish findings about you. If we ever want to run a study, we'll ask you separately, with its own consent form, and saying no will never affect your account.

Only these purposes

These are the only things we use your information for. If we ever want to use it for something new, we'll update this policy and tell you in the app before the change takes effect (see section 12).

We do not:

  • sell or rent your personal information;
  • show you targeted advertising, or let advertisers use your information;
  • share your information with data brokers or marketers;
  • use your listings, messages, or activity to train AI models.

05 Who we share it with

Here is who can see what.

  • Other students: your public profile (name, username, display name, photo, and — if you add them — bio, pronouns, major, and class year), whether your account is currently suspended (never the reason), your listings, and posts you publish for a club. Messages are visible to the person you send them to. When you post a listing, the email on your account is stored with it and can be accessed by other signed-in students. Poll results are shown to students as totals. A club's logo, cover photo, and the event photos it shares are visible to every signed-in student — so if you appear in a club's event photo, other students can see it. Ask the club's officers or us and it will be taken down.
  • Club officers: officers who manage an event or run its check-in can see the name and email of everyone who RSVPs to that event, and whether they checked in. Officers who manage a club's membership can see its member list. Officers with analytics access can see how individual members voted in that club's polls. Officers can't see who follows a club. Officers with analytics access also see totals for their club's events and followers — views, saves, RSVPs, attendance and ratings — but never who viewed, saved or followed. Event ratings and comments are shared anonymously with the organizers: they see the comments and an average rating, never names, and the average stays hidden until at least five people have responded — though in a small event, what a comment says may still hint at who wrote it. Officers must use what they see only for their club's own activities (see our Terms, section 06).
  • Nestrel administrators: the people who run Nestrel can access account information, listings, messages, reports, and activity records in order to verify students, review reports, investigate abuse, and keep the platform working. Administrators can currently open any conversation, not only reported ones. Admin access is limited to accounts we've given an admin role, and every data export is recorded in our activity log.
  • Caldwell University: Nestrel is independent of the university, and we do not share information that identifies individual students with it. If we share anything with the university — for example, as part of a pilot with a university office — it will be totals, such as how many students attended an event, and never figures for a group of fewer than five people. If that ever changes for a particular event or program, we'll tell you there, before you sign up — not after. The one exception is safety: if we believe someone is in danger, we may alert Caldwell's Campus Safety (see "Legal & safety" below).
  • Service providers (subprocessors): companies that help us run Nestrel, listed in section 06.
  • Legal & safety: we may disclose information if required by law (for example, a valid subpoena or court order), or where needed to protect the safety of our users or the public — including alerting law enforcement or Caldwell's Campus Safety when someone may be in danger, or when a serious crime such as threats, violence, fraud or exploitation is reported — or to protect Nestrel against fraud or abuse. We report child sexual abuse material to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC), as U.S. law requires. We share only what the situation requires, and we record these disclosures (below).
  • Business transfer: if Nestrel is transferred to another entity (for example, once Amahle Digital Creatives is formally registered), your information may transfer with it under this policy.

No passing it on

Beyond the people and services listed above, we don't share your personal information with anyone. We do not sell your data or share it with advertisers. When we share totals or statistics instead of personal information, we remove names and anything else that could identify you, and we don't report on groups of fewer than five people.

A record of disclosures

If we ever disclose your personal information to someone outside the categories above — for example, in response to a legal request — we'll record who received it, what was shared, when, and why. You can ask to see that record for your account (section 08), unless the law prevents us from telling you.

08 Your rights & choices

These rights apply to every Nestrel user, wherever you live. New Jersey's Data Privacy Act doesn't yet cover a service Nestrel's size, but the rights it describes — to see, correct and delete your information — are ones we give everyone already. We don't sell personal information or use it for targeted advertising, so there is nothing to opt out of; if your browser sends a Global Privacy Control signal, that is already how we treat you.

  • See your information: ask for a copy of the personal information we hold about you. We'll send it within 45 days — the same window FERPA gives students — and usually much sooner.
  • Correct it: you can edit most of your profile and listings yourself in the app. For anything you can't edit, ask us. If we decide not to make a change, we'll explain why in writing.
  • Delete it: you can delete listings in the app. To delete your whole account, email us and we'll complete it within 30 days of confirming your request (section 07 explains what is kept, and for how long).
  • Take down a photo of you: if you appear in a photo a club has shared, ask the club's officers or us and it will be removed.
  • See our disclosure record: ask whether we've disclosed your information to anyone outside the categories in section 05, and to whom.
  • Raise a concern: if you disagree with how we use your information, tell us. We'll review it and reply in writing.

How to make a request. Email amahledigitalcreatives@gmail.com from your @caldwell.edu address, so we can confirm it's really you. If you no longer have access to that address, tell us and we'll find another way to confirm who you are. Requests are free, and using these rights won't affect your account.

09 Security

We protect your information with measures including:

  • Encryption: data is encrypted in transit (HTTPS) and at rest by our hosting provider, Supabase.
  • Access rules enforced by the database: each account can read only the records it's allowed to see, and those rules are enforced by the database itself — not just by the app's screens.
  • Limited extra access: only accounts we've given an admin role get administrator access. Club officer permissions (managing events, checking people in, viewing analytics) are granted one at a time.
  • Verified accounts: new accounts can only be created with a @caldwell.edu Google account, so Google confirms that the address belongs to you. The database itself refuses sign-ups from any other domain. (Accounts created before Google sign-in confirmed their address by email.)
  • Passwords: any new password must be at least 8 characters, and passwords are stored only in scrambled (hashed) form.
  • Logging: moderation decisions and data exports are recorded in an activity log.

No system is perfectly secure, and as a beta product Nestrel may have vulnerabilities. Please use a strong, unique password and let us know right away if you notice anything suspicious.

If there's a breach. If we learn that someone has gained unauthorized access to your personal information, we'll notify affected users without unreasonable delay — and at least as quickly as New Jersey law requires — and tell you what happened, what information was involved, and what we're doing about it.

10 Age requirement
